Vivi had a little icing on one sleeve.

Rex’s tie had slipped.

The way I’d tied it originally, actually.

We’d come full circle.

They thanked me.

Vivi said it had been exactly what they wanted.

I looked around.

At the collapsed drone.

The recovering best man.

The olive tree now stationed a very long way from the building.

I said:

“Was it?”

She squeezed Rex’s hand.

Then said:

“By the end.”

I understood that.

Sometimes the day you plan is mostly a fence around the day you get.

And sometimes an owl is standing on the fence with your rings.

Later, we signed the paperwork.

In a quiet room.

With no flowers.

No cameras.

No birds.

Portia stood outside the door and checked for birds twice.

I read every line.

Slowly.

Checked the names.

Checked the dates.

Checked that the witnesses were people.

Then I noticed the ladder.

Not physically.

On the form.

Under Occupation of groom, someone had written:

DECORATIVE LADDER.

I looked at Rex.

He looked at the form.

Then he said:

“That might be Chase.”

Apparently Chase had filled out a supplier sheet beside it.

There had been a little copying problem.

We corrected it.

Action-film actor.

Not decorative ladder.

Although I’d seen both of them hold flowers quite successfully.

We finished the signatures.

I checked again.

Then again.

Vivi said:

“Are we definitely married?”

I said yes.

She said:

“To each other?”

I checked once more.

Yes.

Definitely.

They seemed pleased.

I got my four hundred pounds.

My potatoes.

And a little box containing a slice of cake.

The box buzzed once.

I left it outside.

A week later, I saw the wedding photographs.

Beautiful.

Most of them.

There was Rex seeing Vivi at the end of the aisle.

Vivi laughing at the dog drawing.

Their hands.

The rings.

Auntie June looking magnificent with an owl on her shoulder.

And one photograph of me.

Covered in icing.

Holding a bread basket.

The headset was still inside.

You could just see the little microphone sticking out.

The caption said:

THE OFFICIANT WHO SAVED THE DAY.

Very kind.

Not accurate.

Auntie June had saved the day.

I had mainly provided a suitable landing surface.

But the photograph went everywhere.

Millions of people saw it.

Thousands shared it.

Hundreds asked who I was.

And twelve people contacted me about weddings.

I turned them all down.

I’d learned.

Then a thirteenth email arrived.

Very polite.

Small ceremony.

No cameras.

No drones.

No owls.

No bees.

They’d heard I was particularly good with unusual couples.

I thought about it.

Asked whether there would be potatoes.

They said yes.

So I said I’d consider it.

Then they sent the photograph.

Two frogs.

One coat.

I closed the laptop.

And checked the bread basket.
